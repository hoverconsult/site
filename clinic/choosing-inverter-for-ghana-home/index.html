How to choose an inverter for a Ghanaian home

An inverter must handle the connected loads, coordinate available energy and fit the property's electrical arrangement.

Hover Consult Technical Team25 July 20269 minute read

The inverter is the control centre of many backup and solar systems, but a larger kilovolt-ampere number is not automatically better. Selection requires a load study and compatibility checks across batteries, panels, grid supply and distribution.

Continuous power and surge

Add the loads that may operate together, then examine starting currents for refrigerators, pumps and motors. Confirm whether the quoted power is continuous, at what temperature, and for how long surge capability is available.

Battery and solar compatibility

Check battery voltage, supported battery chemistry, charge and discharge current, communication protocols, solar input voltage range, maximum PV power and the number of trackers. Compatibility should be documented.

Ghana planning note: Ask for the inverter model, datasheet and design assumptions before comparing price.

Single-phase and three-phase properties

A three-phase service needs deliberate load allocation and changeover planning. One inverter on one phase does not automatically protect loads on the others, and phase imbalance or generator integration may require specialist design.

Support, environment and expansion

Consider warranty handling, local technical support, software access, enclosure rating, ventilation, operating temperature, noise and parallel capability. Expansion promises should be verified in the manufacturer documentation.

The correct inverter is the one that fits the complete system and operating plan.

Important: This educational article is not a site-specific electrical design, financial valuation or guarantee of system performance. Final decisions require a load assessment, site inspection and appropriately qualified professionals.
