Property & Solar

Solar options for tenants and renters in Ghana

Renters can improve power reliability, but installation rights, portability and safe connection need to be settled first.

Hover Consult Technical Team25 July 20267 minute read

A tenant may want reliable internet, lighting, refrigeration or work equipment without investing permanently in another person's building. The solution should balance service needs with lease terms, landlord consent and safe electrical practice.

Define a portable service

Small packaged systems may support a limited group of appliances and be removable at the end of a tenancy. Check output limits, battery safety and whether the unit needs fixed panel mounting or building alterations.

Get permission for fixed work

Roof mounting, wall penetrations, new distribution boards and changes to property wiring should be agreed with the owner in writing. Clarify restoration obligations and who owns improvements when the tenancy ends.

Ghana planning note: For renters, portability and written consent can be as important as system capacity.

Avoid unsafe connections

Do not back-feed a building through a socket or improvise changeover wiring. A qualified electrician should isolate any fixed backed-up circuits and verify earthing and protection.

Record ownership and maintenance

Keep receipts, serial numbers, warranties and photographs. Agree who insures, maintains and can remove each component, especially where the landlord contributes.

A tenant power solution should be safe to use, clear to own and practical to move.

Important: This educational article is not a site-specific electrical design, financial valuation or guarantee of system performance. Final decisions require a load assessment, site inspection and appropriately qualified professionals.
