Business Power

Solar system sizing for shops and offices in Ghana

Business continuity requires more than adding equipment wattages. Operating schedules and the cost of downtime shape the design.

Hover Consult Technical Team22 July 20269 minute read

A shop may depend on refrigeration, point-of-sale equipment, lights, security and connectivity. An office may prioritise computers, internet, printing and selected air conditioning. Treating every socket as equally important makes the system unnecessarily expensive or operationally confusing.

Separate critical and flexible loads

Identify what must never stop, what can run only during solar hours and what can be excluded during an outage. This produces a priority circuit schedule and a clearer backup target.

Map the working day

Daily energy depends on operating hours. A 300W load used for ten hours consumes more energy than a 1kW appliance used briefly. Refrigeration and pumps also cycle and may have significant starting currents.

Business planning: record opening hours, peak activity, weekend operations, grid outage patterns and planned equipment additions.

Design an expansion route

A growing business should know which parts of the system can expand: inverter parallel capability, battery modules, PV input limits, cable routes and distribution space. Expansion must be engineered, not assumed.

Reliability is a business outcome. The design should protect the activities that generate revenue and preserve customer service.
