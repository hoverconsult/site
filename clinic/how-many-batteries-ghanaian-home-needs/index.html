Battery Planning

How many batteries does a Ghanaian home need?

The useful question is not the number of batteries. It is how many kilowatt-hours your priority loads require.

Hover Consult Technical Team22 July 20268 minute read

Battery quantity can be misleading because batteries differ in voltage, amp-hour rating, chemistry and usable depth of discharge. Begin with the appliances that must remain on and the number of backup hours required.

Calculate energy, not labels

Multiply each appliance wattage by quantity and operating hours. Add the results to obtain watt-hours, then divide by 1,000 for kilowatt-hours. A 100W television used for five hours consumes about 0.5kWh; a refrigerator’s real consumption depends on its cycling behaviour.

Example only: A calculated 4kWh requirement is not automatically a 4kWh battery purchase. Allowances are needed for conversion losses, safe discharge limits, ageing and future load.

Backup and solar charging are connected

A battery bank may provide the requested night backup yet fail to recover during the next day if the solar array is too small or daytime loads consume most generation. Battery and PV sizing should therefore be reviewed together.

Questions that change the answer

  • Which appliances are essential and which can be scheduled?
  • How long must the system operate without grid power?
  • Will air conditioning, pumps or refrigeration be included?
  • Is generator integration available?
  • What future appliances are planned?
A battery count without a load schedule is not a design.
