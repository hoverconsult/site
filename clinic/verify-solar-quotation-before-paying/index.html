Quotation Guidance

How to verify a solar quotation before paying

A quotation should describe a working power system—not merely a shopping list of impressive equipment.

Hover Consult Technical Team22 July 20267 minute read

The most expensive mistake in solar is paying for components before confirming the engineering assumptions behind them. A large inverter does not guarantee long backup, and a long battery description does not prove that the usable energy matches your appliances.

Start with the load schedule

A defensible quotation should identify the appliances to be powered, their operating watts, quantities and expected hours of use. Refrigerators, pumps, motors and air conditioners also need a surge assessment. If the quotation begins with hardware but never defines the load, ask the supplier to explain the basis of design.

Diagnosis should come before quotation. The system must be designed around the client’s problem—not the seller’s available inventory.

Check the five connected layers

Solar quotation review layers and questions
LayerQuestion to ask
InverterCan it carry continuous load and starting surges?
BatteryWhat usable kWh remains after safe discharge limits?
Solar arrayCan it recharge the battery while serving daytime load?
ProtectionAre breakers, isolation, earthing and surge protection included?
InstallationAre cabling, commissioning, labels and handover included?

Read the exclusions

Confirm whether civil work, roof mounting, long cable routes, distribution-board modifications, permits, monitoring, transport and after-sales visits are included. Compare warranties by provider, duration and claim process—not by headline years alone.

Planning note: Any price shown before a load and site assessment remains indicative. Final performance depends on appliance behaviour, weather, installation quality and system settings.
