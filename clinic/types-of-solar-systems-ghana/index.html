Solar Fundamentals

Types of solar systems in Ghana: grid-tied, hybrid and off-grid

The right solar architecture depends on whether the grid is available, whether backup is required and how much independence the owner is willing to fund and manage.

Hover Consult Technical Team25 July 20268 minute read

Solar systems are commonly grouped as grid-tied, hybrid and off-grid. The labels are useful only when their operating behaviour is understood. Two systems with similar panel ratings can behave very differently when the grid fails.

Grid-tied solar

A conventional grid-tied system uses solar while the grid is present and may export energy where an approved arrangement exists. Many grid-tied inverters shut down during an outage for safety, so grid-tied does not automatically mean backup. Confirm current utility and regulatory requirements before planning any export.

Hybrid solar

A hybrid system coordinates solar, batteries and one or more external sources such as the grid or a generator. It can maintain selected loads during an outage, subject to inverter power, battery energy and programmed priorities.

Ghana planning note: Do not assume every solar inverter provides backup. Ask what happens to each circuit when the grid goes off.

Off-grid solar

An off-grid system must meet demand without relying on the public grid. It usually requires tighter load discipline, sufficient storage, provision for low-sun periods and sometimes a generator or other backup source.

Choose by operating objective

If the goal is daytime bill reduction, the design differs from a system intended for overnight backup or full independence. Start with the objective, site conditions and load profile before choosing the label.

System type is an operating decision first and an equipment decision second.

Important: This educational article is not a site-specific electrical design, financial valuation or guarantee of system performance. Final decisions require a load assessment, site inspection and appropriately qualified professionals.
